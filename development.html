This is the app as it is today. Scroll and it walks you through.

1

one-tap walks

Press sanpo and get a short walk of good local places in a sensible order, from where you stand. Choose short, medium or long.

There's a main stop, usually the meal, and the other stops lead up to it. Along the way: shrines and temples, shops, parks, museums, shopping streets, cafés, bakeries and sentos, with walking times and distances between each stop.

Then make it yours: how many are walking, whether you're hungry, what you like and what to leave out, including no alcohol. Swap, remove or move any stop, or add one by kind: a bathroom, a konbini, a shrine. Chains are left out unless you switch them on.

3 min · 240 m

2

searches in japanese

Results come from the places locals go, not the usual tourist lists.

Ask for something to eat or do and get the best open spots near you, with local's picks. Browse by kind, or search by word and get suggestions.

foodcafés & sweetssightsshoppingeverydaydrinks & nightlife

Places that are open now come first, and ratings are adjusted for how many reviews back them. Each place can show a photo, whether it's cash only or cards, and topic chips from reviews like "english menu" or "ticket machine" (a hint, not a promise).

2 min · 80 m

3

free guides

Step-by-step help for eating, shopping, trains and more, with phrases. Free for good.

There are guides for ramen, teishoku, sushi, izakaya, tonkatsu, gyudon, konbini, sento and trains, with tips like how to hold a rice bowl and what to do with a sento milk bottle.

Landing soon? Take Arriving in Japan, a walkthrough in order, from following the signs and your QR code to the booth, baggage, customs and arrivals. There's a before-you-go checklist and an airport guide for leaving, too.

6 min · 430 m

4

show the staff

Phrase cards fill the screen in large Japanese, with the English meaning below and romaji for you to say. Tap one and show it to a station worker or a shop assistant.

The Japanese faces the person across from you. Some are tap-through conversations: you choose, and the staff reply on their own screen.

4 min · 310 m

5

saved places

Save the spots you like, mark the ones you've been to, open any of them in Maps, or copy the Japanese name to show someone.

Found a place somewhere else? Paste a map link and walk to it.

and the small things.

on a walk

Loop back to where you started. Open the whole walk in Google Maps or Apple Maps for directions. Share a walk by link, open one someone shared, or pick up an unfinished one where you left it.

A "?" on each stop explains the kind of place in plain words: machi-chūka, dagashiya and so on. Places you've marked "been here" are left out unless you ask.

finding places

Hand-picked special stops are marked on results and walks. Busy districts like Shibuya and Asakusa come with honest notes about where the quieter streets are.

Bathroom help tells you where toilets are and aren't, and there's a rubbish guide for the rest.

at the place

Seating notes, and a warning when a place may need a booking. Menu words in Japanese for dishes found in the reviews, and Google's review summary, shown as Google's own.

quiet by design

A short welcome asks what you like, how many of you there are and how long you're staying, and skips alcohol if you want. A first tour shows you around once. "Your Tokyo" only counts things you actually pressed.

There's a dark mode, no streaks, no nagging, and hints that appear once.

Ratings and hours come from Google Maps. Review summaries are Google's own. Strongest in Tokyo.
